Clarity Skills

An event notice may say that a waiting list is available without explaining whether applicants should come to the venue. What seems obvious to the writer may leave a first-time participant unsure what to do.

AI has the same problem. It tends to bring loosely connected concepts together into a system of its own, then hand the user a planning document that is often hard to follow.

Clarity is a collection of writing skills for AI assistants. They help authors supply the context readers need to understand a proposal and judge its merits.

A question about the waiting list is best answered where the term appears. nutshell keeps that explanation in place. Readers can find out how a place is offered without leaving the notice to search for a separate set of rules.

This does not mean withholding essential information. The deadline still belongs in the main text. Readers familiar with the process need not work through every detail again.

Understanding a rule does not settle whether it is a good one. Consider this example of daily quests in a game. They may give players a reason to return, but they can also make playing feel like a daily obligation.

Keep daily quests for three daysProposed

In this example, a set of quests appears at midnight each day and expires 24 hours later. A player who skips a day returns to find those quests gone.

Completing quests therefore depends on logging in every day. Even a player with time to catch up cannot recover the missed opportunity.

The proposal keeps the daily schedule but makes each set available for 72 hours. The reward for each quest stays the same.

Players could miss a day or two and complete the quests that remain available when they return. Older quests would still expire, so there would be no unlimited backlog.

The change would require saving progress for three sets of quests and checking what happens at midnight, at expiry, and when rewards are claimed. Players may earn more rewards overall; the effect on progression would need assessment.
